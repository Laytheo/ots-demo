Book club tonight. We were three weeks into [[Warranted Christian Belief]] and I had been waiting for tonight, because I had a particular objection lined up and I wanted to deploy it.

The objection is the one everyone has when they first meet [[Reformed Epistemology]]. If theistic belief can be properly basic, why not belief in the Great Pumpkin? Why not any old thing somebody happens to find themselves believing? It feels, on first encounter, like a knockdown move. I was ready.

What happened in the discussion is that the question shifted under me before I got to ask it. T. brought up the part where [[Alvin Plantinga]] is talking about the conditions under which a belief-forming faculty produces *warranted* output, and she made the point, in her own words, that the question is not whether *any* basic belief is permitted, but whether belief in God is the kind of thing that could be the output of a properly functioning faculty in the right environment. That is where the work is. The Great Pumpkin objection, she said, is a question about the architecture, and Plantinga has answered it, even if you don't like the answer.

I sat with my objection in my pocket and didn't pull it out. Not because I was embarrassed, but because pulling it out would have been answering a question that was no longer the one being asked.

Walking home I tried to figure out whether I had been beaten or merely *moved*. I think I was moved. The shape of the disagreement I have with Plantinga is not the shape I had imagined when I sat down for the first chapter. I am not closer to agreeing with him. I am closer to understanding what we actually disagree about.

There is a particular kind of evening that earns its night-cap, and after we broke up T. and I walked the two blocks over to [[McAllister's Tavern]] for one. We did not keep arguing. We talked about her sister and the strange weather and a film neither of us had seen yet. The disagreement could wait. The night-cap could not.
